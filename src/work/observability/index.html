Seeing problems first

Finding out about issues from a dashboard, not from a support ticket.

The situation

Problems were too often noticed after they reached users. Metrics and logs existed, but they were spread across tools, and alerts didn’t always point at what actually needed attention.

What I built

  • Established observability across Prometheus, Grafana, CloudWatch and the ELK stack, so metrics and logs for a service can be read together.
  • Built Grafana dashboards and predictive alerts that flag anomalies before they turn into outages.
  • Handled incident response and root cause analysis, documented lessons learned and followed through on the fixes.

How it flows

  1. Metrics and logs
  2. Dashboards
  3. Alert
  4. Respond
  5. Root cause review
  6. Fix and document

Decisions I made and why

Every alert needs an owner and an action

An alert that nobody acts on trains people to ignore alerts. If it fires, someone should know what to do.

Watch trends, not just thresholds

Predictive alerts catch a problem that is getting worse before it crosses the line where users notice.

Reviews are about the system

Root cause reviews look for what in the system allowed the problem, then fix that, so the same incident doesn’t come back next month.

What changed

Downtime went down 25%, and anomalies started showing up on a dashboard before they showed up in a support queue.

What I’d do next

Add service-level objectives for the most important services, so alerting is based on what users experience.

Stack Prometheus, Grafana, CloudWatch, ELK
